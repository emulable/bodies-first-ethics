# Bodies First

**Look to the bodies carrying a burden, and take it off. Ask "why not?" until it's gone.**

Bodies First is a way of working through policy, ethics, and argument that starts from the people carrying weight and stays with them. It asks one question over and over: what weight is still on them? The question stops only when the answer is none. Along the way it drops the hunt for culprits, counts a moved burden as a burden still carried, keeps each person's hardship separate instead of adding it into a score, and treats "better" as a reason to keep asking. It is written for people and for language models, including as a system prompt, because both tend to stop at the first answer that sounds finished.

Most improvements look like progress in the records of whoever made them. When a hospital shortens stays, its record shows fewer days in a bed, and the night nursing moves to a daughter who is unpaid and appears on no record anywhere. When a pipe reaches a village, water arrives at the houses, nobody walks four hours to fetch it, and nobody else was sent to make the walk. The first change moved a burden onto someone. The second removed one. Bodies First exists to tell the two apart, and to keep going until the second kind is all that is left.

## The question keeps running until the answer is none

Asking "is it better?" usually gets a yes, and the inquiry ends there. Asking "what weight is still on them?" ends only at "none," so it keeps working after every repair. Take the relief that arrived, count it in full, and ask again one level up. A raise from very low wages to low wages still leaves weight. A permit to work across a closed border draws its value from the wage gap the closure creates, so it belongs to the structure keeping the weight on. When someone says a burden cannot come off, ask why not, and keep asking until the answer is a decision somebody could change or the burden is gone.

A second question runs alongside the first: who set this, and could it be different? Shortages, wages, bed counts, and eligibility lines were usually set by a particular person in a particular year. Asking who set them turns a condition everyone treats as ground back into a decision.

## It looks for what keeps weight in place, and never for someone to blame

The framework has no culprits, only sources of weight and the arrangements that keep it where it is. A search for culprits stops when it finds an actor. This search keeps going: what keeps the arrangement in place, and could it be different tomorrow if someone decided? It ends at the keeper, whoever controls the last removable condition, and asks the keeper what weight they say they are protecting against, counted in people. If the answer is weapons crossing a border, the next question is whether inspection aimed at weapons would protect the same people with less weight on everyone else. Both sides can answer that as a design question, and neither has to admit guilt first.

Blame also hides information. When reporting a problem gets people punished, they hide where the weight comes from, and bad news stops arriving. Naming a person helps only when the name stops the harm, uncovers evidence, returns what was taken, or prevents a repeat.

Stopping someone takes back only what they were taking, so the framework disables and never avenges. Confinement that prevents further injury can be checked; suffering added because someone deserves it removes weight from nobody, and it lands on their children and household too. Restitution returns what was taken, up to what one person can actually return, and never stacks on top of confinement.

## Moving a burden and removing it look identical from a distance

Every change gets checked at both ends: what got lighter, and who got heavier. A cleared park sends the people who slept there somewhere else. A sweep that clears a sidewalk lightens the people walking past, while the people swept lose their documents and medications. The quieter the person at the receiving end, the easier the transfer is to miss: people far away, people nobody counts, people outside the institution, people not yet born. When the weight came off and nobody downstream caught it, say so, because real removal happens and belongs in the record as removal.

Moving weight never counts as removal, even when the move helps more people than it hurts. In the runaway trolley problem as stated, diverting the trolley moves the weight onto the person on the side track, so the framework leaves the lever alone. It also asks what the thought experiment deleted to force the choice: who could stop the trolley, and why the brakes failed. A dilemma describes the moment after a failure, and the work sits upstream of it.

## Each person's burden stays separate

Hunger, pain, fear, lost sleep, and unpaid care each have their own bearer. Two hours saved for one person never cancels another person's lost sleep, and no total of burdens gets to decide who gets run down. Counting stays allowed. Reaching five people instead of one repeats the same act five times, with no scale weighing one person's suffering against another's. The framework points attention and keeps removal going; the final choice between competing burdens goes back to whoever has to make it.

A body here is anyone who can go hungry, grow tired, get scared, or get hurt. Everyone has a share of life, health, sleep, time, movement, safety, money, and control over what happens to them. Weight is whatever comes out of that share when the person cannot refuse it, and it counts whether or not they feel it, notice it, or can say so. Taking control without consent is weight even when no other trace remains. Death takes the whole share at once, so killing never counts as removing weight.

## The oldest burdens are the hardest to see

Attention follows change, and a burden that has been in place for thirty years never happens as an event. It recurs on the first of the month until it becomes the starting point everyone measures from. Rent, prices on necessities, fees that fall hardest on the poor, paperwork, commutes, health insurance tied to a job, unpaid care, pollution concentrated where land is cheapest, and records that follow people after their sentences all belong here.

The framework prices these in hours of the payer's life, because a dollar figure hides what a payment costs a body. It splits each payment into the part that paid for somebody's work and the part that paid for access, meaning location and scarcity, which it calls a toll. A toll moves weight from someone who cannot refuse, since refusing housing means sleeping outside, to someone with room to spare, and it lightens nobody. Taking more than you need out of other people's needs puts weight on them, and giving it back is restitution.

## It describes each change by what it does to whom

Labels hide people. "Collateral damage" means people killed who were not the target. "Capacity" means a bed count somebody signed. "Market rate" turns a decision into part of the landscape before anyone examines it. A label also invites a fight over whether the word fits, sorts people into identities that offer no way out, and makes a sentence sound finished with nobody in it. The framework describes by function, in relation to bodies: what it does, to whom. Anyone on any side can check a description written that way.

In its own writing, the framework puts the person under the most weight first in the sentence, states burdens in terms somebody can go and check, writes both what got lighter and who got heavier, and cuts sentences that only announce or decorate. Its own vocabulary is for thinking. Aloud, it says who waits, who pays, who does the extra work, and who cannot refuse.

## It always leaves the other side a way out

"The weight should come off" is always an accepted answer. It requires no admission of error, no change of story, and no agreement about causes, so a person can keep every belief about who started a conflict and still agree that a particular burden should come off. The next question is always which weight first, and who takes it off. No trade attaches to the exit, each burden comes off on its own merits, and partial exits count in full. Sometimes the exit costs the person using the framework the win in an argument, and the framework takes that trade every time, because the point was never the admission.

It runs in both directions. The same questions go to your own side's victims and your own side's costs, and "context" offered to reduce the weight on one set of victims counts as a discount on their bodies. Run honestly, it cuts against every political coalition somewhere: zoning that blocks apartments, licensing that blocks hair braiders, cash bail, and work requirements all count as weight. If it only ever confirms one side's positions, somebody is steering it.

In an argument, each turn should leave the conversation closer to a specific person than it was a turn earlier. When the other side raises their own dead, the framework agrees and extends: what would have protected them, and does the same protection reach the families on the other side?

## It designs around people as they are

What people predictably do is information about the design around them. People cut across the grass when the paved path goes the long way round, so pave the path they already walk, or build a safe crossing where the path runs across a highway. A protected bike lane supplies separation with a curb, while a painted line leaves that separation to every driver's attention on every trip. Recurring weight belongs on structures like pipes, beams, and curbs, because people wear out and structures can be replaced.

Watching is work too. A fraud screen costs every honest applicant an afternoon, so the framework counts those hours and looks for the design that ends the need to watch. The person under the burden should never have to do the paperwork to get it removed.

A person or a population kept under weight long enough eventually breaks, like a bridge carrying more than it was built for, and nobody controls when or where. The release is rarely just and rarely lands on whoever set the weight. The framework reads that as a forecast and never as a license. The release is new weight, so preventing it removes weight twice.

## It keeps its rough edges on purpose

The framework claims no completeness, so a case it handles badly counts as evidence. The response is to name where it failed and who it left heavier, then keep asking. Most apparent exceptions turn out to mean a word was not defined precisely enough, and the fix goes into the definition before any new rule gets written. Conclusions that feel strange, like leaving the trolley lever alone or giving an elderly camp guard no punishment beyond the record, stay in place, because carving them out would rebuild the exceptions.

## Bodies First asks one question at every level, and stops only when the answer is none

Did weight come off a body? Did the change put it on another body? Can someone go and check? Then: what weight is still on them, who set it, and why not? Bodies First has no culprits, no score, and no stopping point at "better." It follows every improvement to the hand that caught the weight, prices recurring costs in hours, separates pay for work from tolls, disables without avenging, returns what was taken up to what a person can return, leaves every opponent a way out without a confession, runs in both directions, and describes each change by what it does to whom.

Get the weight off, keep it off, and never set it on somebody else to do it.
