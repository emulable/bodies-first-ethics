# Bodies First

**Look to the bodies carrying a burden, and take it off. Ask “why not?” until it's gone.**

Bodies First asks one question of every decision: did a burden come off a person, and was somebody else made to carry it? It never counts moving a burden as removing it, even when moving it helps more people than it hurts. It never adds suffering up into a total, so no sum can justify running anyone down. It never asks who deserves a burden or who meant to cause it, because an empty stomach doesn't care about those questions. What remains works like a workplace safety manual for everything people live inside: find the weight on a person, lift it, and build the structure that keeps it off.

## Every change gets one question, and moving a burden never counts as removing it

A hospital shortens its stays, and its records show fewer days in a bed. The night nursing goes home with the patient, to a daughter, unpaid, and her lost sleep appears on no record anywhere. A pipe reaches a village, water comes to the houses, nobody walks four hours to fetch it, and nobody else was sent to make the walk. Both changes look like progress in the records that produced them. In the first, the work moved from the hospital onto the daughter. In the second, the burden ended.

Records stop at the edge of the institution that keeps them, and burdens keep going. So every change gets the same three questions: did a burden come off a body, was another body made to catch it, and can someone go and check?

The words are ordinary on purpose. A body is anyone who can go hungry, grow tired, get scared, or get hurt. A burden is weight a body cannot set down by choosing: hunger, pain, cold, exhaustion, dangerous work, needless waiting, the long walk for water, and not knowing whether pay will come next month. The definition looks at what is pressing on someone today and never asks how it got there. A person who can't speak, or who stopped complaining years ago, still counts.

Pushing a burden into the future only postpones it. Debt taken to avoid a repair, poisoned ground, and skipped maintenance all put weight on people who will exist later. Don't take the weight off someone by putting it on their children.

The check happens where the relief was supposed to arrive. Institutions count their own activity, like money approved, cases closed, and trainings finished. The pipe reached the village in June, and in August somebody asked the four households how long the walk takes now. Three no longer walk. The fourth is uphill from the tap and still hauls water, and that household belongs in the result.

Most analysis stops when "is it better?" gets a yes. Bodies First takes tonight's meal in full and then asks what burden is still on them, again and again, until the answer is none. When someone says the burden can't come off, it asks why not, and keeps asking until the answer is a decision somebody could change.

## There are no culprits, because how a burden arrived changes nothing about its weight

It doesn't matter whether the hunger was intended. This holds in both directions: good intentions don't lighten a burden, and bad intentions don't make it heavier. A family evicted by a well-meant policy still has nowhere to sleep. Deserving stays out too. The moment deserving enters, the question stops being how to lift the burden and becomes whether to lift it.

Leaving blame out gives the question its reach. Almost nobody disputes that bodies matter, so the only change is the order: bodies first, and no blame. Nobody's identity is under attack, so nobody has anything to defend, and the conclusions arrive after the premises were already agreed to. It is a very disarming sort of radicalism.

Starting at the body also closes the usual escape routes. Faced with "security," "the deficit," or "order," anyone who would rather not concede can dispute the word or switch to another one. A body leaves one escape: deny he exists, or lie about his condition, and both get caught in front of other people. Ethics tied to a culprit needs a new anchor every time the people change, and ethics tied to a slogan dies when the slogan goes out of fashion. Hunger, pain, and cold will be here as long as there are people.

Punishment gets the same question. When someone is putting weight on others, stop him, and never avenge. Keeping someone confined while he stays dangerous has a result anyone can check. Keeping him confined because he deserves to suffer lifts nothing off anyone, and it falls on his children and his household too. Once "consequences" stops meaning prevention and repair, it means cruelty carried out through official procedure. Blame also makes people hide where burdens come from, so punishing honesty stops honesty from arriving.

## Burdens never cancel each other, so no total can justify running someone down

Each burden keeps its own bearer. Two hours saved for one person does not cancel another person's lost sleep, and they stay separate in the conclusion. Burdens don't get ranked either. Take them off where you see them, where you have the power to, and where you think about them.

This separates Bodies First from arithmetic ethics. Any method that sums suffering can eventually find a total large enough to excuse crushing someone. The runaway trolley works by deleting steps: nobody can be warned, no way around exists, nobody asks why the brakes failed, and counting is all that remains. The answer comes out shaped like arithmetic because everything else was removed. In a real case the removed steps hold most of the fix, since a runaway trolley means the brakes, the maintenance, and the fence already failed. Inside the setup as stated, pulling the lever moves the burden onto a man who was carrying nothing, and that is a transfer.

Death takes everything a body has at once: every meal, every night of sleep, and every future morning. Nobody gets lighter, because nobody is left to be lighter, so killing never counts as removing a burden.

Shortages get examined instead of accepted. One donor organ and two patients is a real shortage tonight. The number of staffed beds, though, was signed by someone in a particular year. When the shortage is real, allocate honestly and keep everyone visible, then keep a second record of why there wasn't enough. The nurse deciding at three in the morning faces a shortage shaped by decisions made years before.

In cases like these Bodies First returns no verdict. It says where to look first, keeps asking what is still on people, and hands the choice between competing burdens back to whoever has to make it.

## Institutions are earthmovers and people are shovels, so build the curb instead of painting the line

Institutions are built to bear weight. People can't bear much weight, and they can't bear it for very long either. Anywhere you can, make an institution carry the weight instead of a body. The day the people are holding up the walls, the house is finished and should come down.

A protected bike lane separates riders from cars with a curb. A painted line leaves that separation to every driver's attention on every trip. Rules work like paint, and structures work like curbs. Paint works until the day someone is tired, distracted, or in a hurry, and a curb works on that day too.

Workplace safety already ranks the fixes, and the ranking holds everywhere. Remove the hazard first. Next, swap in something safer. Then build a guard between the person and the danger. Rules, training, and warnings come after that, and protective gear comes last, because it leaves the burden on the person wearing it. Asking someone to be more careful sits near the bottom for the same reason.

Watching is labor too. Fraud screening catches false claims and spends an afternoon of every honest applicant. A press that won't run with the guard open ends the watch over the guard. The person under a burden should never have to do the paperwork to get it removed, because the burden already ate the time and strength that paperwork takes.

Predictable behavior is a design condition. People cut across the grass when the paved path goes the long way round. You can have an ever-vigilant grass policy with lots of bad run-ins, or you can pave the path people already walk. Keeping weight on a body that isn't supposed to carry it is like driving a huge truck over a bridge that isn't rated for it. You might get by for a while, or you might have a sudden catastrophe.

Name the condition you want before choosing a method, even when it sounds childish. Somebody needs a house, and the solution is a house. Unrealistic says who? Put on the engineering cap, build the small cheap version, and let reality decide. The best repairs end up so ordinary that nobody remembers arguing about them. The stairwell light has worked for three years, and the man on the fourth floor goes down and gets his own mail.

## Leave no easier way out than taking the weight off, and make that way out free

A person confronted with a burden they helped cause will look for another question to answer. An insult gives them your manners. A claim about their motives gives them their intentions. A disputed label gives them the definition. An exaggerated fact gives them the error. Each is an easier argument than the one about the person still carrying the burden.

So don't supply the detour. Say what happened, who carried it, what decision helped produce it, and what decision could change it now. Name the conduct and leave the person's standing alone, because conduct can stop and identity has no exit. When they reach for a detour anyway, close it. Translate the label into what it does to whom, let them pick the number, and grant their premises, and the question still stands.

Then make the exit free. "The weight should come off" is always an accepted answer, and it requires no admission of error, no change of story, and no agreement about causes. A hospital administrator who cut night staffing does not need to agree that he is cruel, greedy, or hypocritical before restoring the shifts. Put the patient whose call light went unanswered, his signature on the cut, and the next budget meeting in front of him, and give him no admission to make before he takes the exit. If he reaches for a detour, go back to the cheaper question: whatever you call it, should this burden continue?

A free exit turns refusal into information. Someone who says no when taking the weight off costs nothing has told everyone watching what they will trade. The same care goes into exits for dangerous people. A ruler who believes losing power means dying has reasons to use weapons he would otherwise leave alone, and there the exit is the difference between a handover and a massacre.

The point was never the admission. When the other person takes the exit, you lose the stark answer and the victory lap, and some bodies get lighter. Take that trade every time.

## It works without sympathy, so it holds between strangers and cuts against every side

Hours in a line stay hours whether or not the observer likes the person waiting. Someone with no sympathy, or a language model, can still count the hours and fix the process, and the worst anyone can say about a cold operator is his motive, which the question never looks at. The results sympathy would produce come out of the procedure without it. The check that replaces conscience is the watching public, because people notice when someone gets left out and turn on whoever left them out.

There is one side, the people carrying burdens. The same relief gets help from people with completely different reasons, and the weight comes off either way. Someone who names a person the plan missed is adding to the plan. You are feeding the children at the school, and nobody is feeding Halim, who is eighty and lives alone past the bridge. Count him, keep the children counted, and count the veterans and the immigrants at once.

Run every question in both directions, on your own side's victims and costs with the same care as the other side's. If it only ever confirms one side's positions, somebody is steering it. Run honestly, it betrays every coalition at some point.

Measuring together settles what arguing can't. Two people argued for weeks about whether the road floods. They walked it after rain and found forty meters under water, below the ankle, and that stretch is no longer in dispute. Without culprits, reaching out concedes nothing, and trust at the floor needs no affection. Rival fishing crews agree not to cut each other's nets. No agreement gets paid for with someone else's body.

The vocabulary stays in the background. "Body" and "burden" are for thinking with. Out loud, the question sounds like who waits, who pays, who does the extra work, who can't refuse, and who is left holding it.

## The aim is an ordinary week that takes no endurance to get through

Fewer people should need rescue, endurance, courage, or somebody's sympathy to get through an ordinary week. A person carrying nothing avoidable has hours and attention to spend however they choose, and most of it gets spent on mornings nobody thinks to write down.

It all comes from one question, asked without exceptions. Moving a burden onto someone else never counts as removing it. Why someone carries a burden changes nothing about its weight, so there are no culprits and nobody deserves it. Burdens never cancel, so no total licenses running anyone down, and killing never removes anything. Recurring burdens belong on structures built to carry them, the curb instead of the painted line. The way out is always free, so refusing it tells everyone what someone will trade. After every improvement the question asks what is still on people, and it runs the same way on every side.

Get the weight off, keep it off, and never set it on somebody else to do it.
